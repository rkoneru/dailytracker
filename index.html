                      Capacity Planning

                      From demand to a plan the pool can actually deliver. The process below is a reference; the numbers under it are read live off this workspace's projects, resources and bookings.

                      1 The process

                      From demand to balanced execution. No data on this card — it is a reference, not a form.
                      1. 1

                        Capture demand

                        Collect the work that wants doing: projects, tasks and the estimates behind them. This is and each project's own Task Tracker.

                      2. 2

                        Analyse capacity

                        Understand who is available, at what capacity, and what leave and other commitments already take off the top. This is the pool and its Availability tab.

                      3. 3

                        Compare & prioritise

                        Match demand against what is actually available. Where the two do not fit, decide what defers, shrinks or gets more people rather than letting every project run 5% behind.

                      4. 4

                        Allocate resources

                        Book the right people to the right work, time-phased rather than open-ended. This is .

                        Execute & monitor

                        Keep the plan honest as it runs: watch utilisation, catch overload before it becomes a missed date, adjust when the picture changes.

                      Common capacity gaps

                      • Too many projects running at once, none of them fully staffed
                      • Estimates that assumed nobody would ever be on leave
                      • A skill only one person has, booked on two projects at once
                      • Allocations nobody revisits once the project is under way
                      • Priorities that change faster than the plan can be re-run

                      When demand exceeds capacity

                      1. Reassess priorities — which projects actually deliver the most value?
                      2. Reduce or split scope — can the project be smaller, or phased?
                      3. Shift timelines — can it start later, or run longer?
                      4. Add resources — internally, or from outside?
                      5. Defer or stop the lowest-value work rather than spread everyone thinner

                      Best practice

                      • Plan against realistic capacity, not a full week per person
                      • Count all the work — projects, BAU and support, not just the visible plan
                      • Book against skills, not just headcount
                      • Revisit allocations on a schedule, not only when something breaks
                      • Keep one shared, transparent view rather than a spreadsheet per team

                      Sync & Team

                      This app works entirely offline and keeps your projects in this browser. Connecting a Supabase project adds a copy on a server you own, so the same data reaches your other devices and teammates can be given access.

                      1 · Connect a Supabase project

                      Create a free project at supabase.com, run supabase/schema.sql from this repository in its SQL editor, then paste the project URL and anon key from Project Settings → API. Both are stored in this browser only. The anon key is meant to be public — row level security is what protects your data.

                      2 · Sign in

                      We email you a sign-in link — there is no password to choose or store. Open it on every device you want to keep in sync.
