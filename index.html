                                                    Overload

                                                    • High resource utilisation (over 85%)
                                                    • Demand running ahead of capacity (gap over +10%)
                                                    • Overloaded roles climbing
                                                    • On-time delivery falling

                                                    Underutilisation

                                                    • Low resource utilisation (under 70%)
                                                    • Demand running behind capacity (gap under -10%)
                                                    • Buffer capacity high (over 20%)
                                                    • Throughput falling with people still free

                                                    Healthy balance

                                                    • Utilisation in the 70–85% band
                                                    • Demand-vs-capacity gap within ±10%
                                                    • On-time delivery in the 85–95% band
                                                    • Buffer capacity in the 10–20% band

                                                    Monthly review checklist

                                                    A five-step check to keep the portfolio on track. Look at these together — one number out of band is a watch item, several moving the same way is the thing to act on.

                                                    1. Review utilisation by person, not just the average
                                                    2. Compare demand against capacity for the weeks ahead, not just now
                                                    3. Check on-time delivery and throughput together
                                                    4. Look at forecast accuracy — is the plan learning from what actually happened?
                                                    5. Confirm buffer capacity is enough to absorb the next surprise

                                                    Common capacity gaps

                                                    • Too many projects running at once, none of them fully staffed
                                                    • Estimates that assumed nobody would ever be on leave
                                                    • A skill only one person has, booked on two projects at once
                                                    • Allocations nobody revisits once the project is under way
                                                    • Priorities that change faster than the plan can be re-run

                                                    When demand exceeds capacity

                                                    1. Reassess priorities — which projects actually deliver the most value?
                                                    2. Reduce or split scope — can the project be smaller, or phased?
                                                    3. Shift timelines — can it start later, or run longer?
                                                    4. Add resources — internally, or from outside?
                                                    5. Defer or stop the lowest-value work rather than spread everyone thinner

                                                    Best practice

                                                    • Plan against realistic capacity, not a full week per person
                                                    • Count all the work — projects, BAU and support, not just the visible plan
                                                    • Book against skills, not just headcount
                                                    • Revisit allocations on a schedule, not only when something breaks
                                                    • Keep one shared, transparent view rather than a spreadsheet per team

                                                    Planning Layers

                                                    Three horizons, cascading into each other: where the practice is going, how that becomes coordinated work, and what actually happens today. A number that cannot be traced back up this chain to a strategic priority is busywork, however well it is executed.

                                                    01

                                                    Strategic Planning

                                                    Long-term direction — sets vision, defines where and how to compete.

                                                    3–5 years

                                                    Key focus

                                                    • Market position and differentiation
                                                    • Competitive advantage
                                                    • Vision and growth opportunities

                                                    Owned by

                                                    • Leadership sets direction
                                                    • Sponsors and the steering committee agree priorities
                                                    • Governance validates the choice

                                                    Best practice

                                                    • A handful of priorities, not a wishlist
                                                    • Decide what you will not do, not only what you will
                                                    • Revisit as the market moves, not on a fixed clock

                                                    Common mistakes

                                                    • Vague or overly broad goals
                                                    • Avoiding the hard trade-offs
                                                    • Chasing every opportunity at once
                                                    02

                                                    Tactical Planning

                                                    The bridge to execution — turns strategy into coordinated initiatives.

                                                    1–3 years

                                                    Key focus

                                                    • Team alignment around initiatives
                                                    • Cross-team resource coordination
                                                    • Progress toward the strategic goals above

                                                    Owned by

                                                    • Programme and delivery leads coordinate
                                                    • Cross-functional owners align
                                                    • Middle management runs it — this is , and territory

                                                    Best practice

                                                    • Roadmaps with real milestones — see
                                                    • One accountable owner per initiative
                                                    • Regular checkpoint reviews, not just a kickoff and a retro

                                                    Common mistakes

                                                    • Plans disconnected from the strategy that funded them
                                                    • More initiatives than the pool has capacity for — see
                                                    • Poor coordination across teams sharing the same people
                                                    03

                                                    Operational Planning

                                                    Short-term execution — where the work actually happens, day to day.

                                                    Days–12 months

                                                    Key focus

                                                    • Consistent delivery and quality
                                                    • Day-to-day process efficiency
                                                    • Frontline execution

                                                    Owned by

                                                    • Team leads manage the daily work
                                                    • Supervisors oversee completion
                                                    • This project's own and

                                                    Best practice

                                                    • Standard steps for repeated work
                                                    • Track the handful of indicators that matter — see
                                                    • Keep the plan realistic, not a full week booked per person

                                                    Common mistakes

                                                    • Mistaking activity for progress
                                                    • Losing sight of the strategy above
                                                    • Overloading the team beyond what capacity supports

                                                    From strategy to execution

                                                    Each layer asks the question the one below it has to answer.
                                                    1. 1

                                                      Define the top-level goal

                                                      Leadership sets one to three priorities with a measurable outcome — "reach 30% market share in two years," not "grow the business." Ask: what has to be true for this to happen?

                                                    2. 2

                                                      Break it into initiatives

                                                      Each priority becomes two to four tactical initiatives, each with a deadline and a named owner — tracked as with their own . Ask: what does the team do every week to make this happen?

                                                    3. 3

                                                      Translate into daily execution

                                                      Each initiative becomes concrete and the two or three that show whether it is working. Ask: what does this look like day to day?

                                                    People & Roles

                                                    Only an administrator can assign roles here. An administrator is the project’s owner, or someone the owner has granted the right to run the workspace — usually the engagement manager.

                                                    Access is what somebody may read and write, and it is enforced by the server. Job role decides which pages the app offers them, and it is not a security boundary — see What is actually enforced below. Client partner is: it decides who the database will return use cases and ROI models to. Only the owner can grant it, and a delegated admin cannot edit anyone who holds it.

                                                    Sync & Team

                                                    This app works entirely offline and keeps your projects in this browser. Connecting a Supabase project adds a copy on a server you own, so the same data reaches your other devices and teammates can be given access.

                                                    1 · Connect a Supabase project

                                                    Create a free project at supabase.com, run supabase/schema.sql from this repository in its SQL editor, then paste the project URL and anon key from Project Settings → API. Both are stored in this browser only. The anon key is meant to be public — row level security is what protects your data.

                                                    2 · Sign in

                                                    We email you a sign-in link — there is no password to choose or store. Open it on every device you want to keep in sync.
